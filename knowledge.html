Knowledge

Frameworks, not just opinions

Explainers and visual systems I've built to make clinical content survive contact with a real patient — in their own language, at their own reading level.

Framework

S.M.I.L.E.

An AI-assisted preventive-health education visual system designed for Kannada-first learners. It uses structured mnemonics and culturally relevant analogies to simplify pharmacology concepts that are normally taught only in English — built for Tier-2 and Tier-3 students and first-generation digital learners.

Method

Plain-language translation layer

The working method behind most of my writing: take the evidence at full clinical fidelity, then build a second layer that a patient can act on — the interpretation layer that sits between data and decision. Not a simplification of the science; a translation of it.

Health literacyEvidence synthesisPatient education

Note

Why AI is a tool, not the clinician

Technology should improve how knowledge is communicated, understood and applied — not replace the judgement of a healthcare professional. The useful role for AI here is simplification and reach: helping the same clinical message land in five languages and at three reading levels.

Note

Adherence is a communication outcome

Most non-adherence isn't stubbornness — it's a comprehension failure somewhere upstream. If a patient cannot restate what the medicine is for and when to take it, the counselling didn't land, however complete it was on paper.
